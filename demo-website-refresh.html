Six changes, in the order I would make them.
1A grey box where a picture should beThe old page has an empty placeholder that was never filled in. A real picture goes there, sized so it loads fast and still looks sharp on a phone.
2The heading welcomed you instead of saying what the shop doesSomebody arriving from a search wants to know they are in the right place. It now names the job and the town, in a typeface you can read at arm's length instead of a serif squeezed to 13 pixels.
3Leftover filler text from the templateThe lorem ipsum was never replaced. It is gone, and the space now answers the question people actually arrive with, with room to breathe around it.
4One clear button instead of "click here"A grey system button that names nothing gets pressed by nobody. One button, in one colour used nowhere else on the page, saying what happens when you press it.
5The layout was built for a desktop screenText floating around a fixed-width image breaks the moment the screen is narrow. The three things the shop sells now sit in a row that folds into a column on a phone, which is where most people will see it.
6Opening hours were a phone call away, and the date said 2015The one thing people came for is now the first thing they see, as a small badge at the top. And the year at the bottom says when the page was last touched, because it was.
